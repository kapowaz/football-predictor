[Arsenal Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) is an English professional [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Holloway, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holloway,_London). The club was formed in [Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich) in 1886 as Dial Square before it was shortly renamed to Royal Arsenal, and then Woolwich Arsenal in 1893. They became the first southern member admitted into [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) in 1893, having spent their first four seasons solely participating in cup tournaments and [friendlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_game#Association_football). The club's name was shortened to Arsenal in 1914, a year after moving to [Highbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_Stadium). In spite of finishing fifth in the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) in 1915, Arsenal rejoined the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) at the expense of local rivals [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) when football resumed after the First World War. Since that time, they have not fallen below the first tier of the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system) and hold the record for the longest uninterrupted time in the top flight.

In the 1930s, Arsenal were the dominant side of England, winning five [league championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_English_football_champions) and two [FA Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup). Their fortunes waned, but the club soon enjoyed infrequent periods of success, including [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup) triumph and a first league and cup [double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_(association_football)#England) in the 1970s. During the late 1980s, Arsenal had built a side that threatened [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.)'s league dominance, and performed greatly in cup competitions. The club played an active role in the formation of the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) in 1992, won the FA Cup in 1993 and the [European Cup Winners Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cup_Winners_Cup) in 1994 and two doubles followed in 1998 and 2002. Arsenal made league history in [2003–04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_in_English_football) when they became the first team in a 38-game season to go unbeaten. In the 2000s, Arsenal were finalists in both the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup) and [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League), and have since equalled [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_C.F.)'s record for most consecutive seasons in the latter competition.

As of the end of the [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_in_English_football) season, the club's first team have spent 108 seasons in the top division of English football, and 13 in the second. Their worst league finish to date is 10th in the second tier, their placing at the end of the [1896–97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896%E2%80%9397_in_English_football) season. Arsenal's best-ever start to a Premier League season came in [2022–23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_in_English_football), when they won nine of their first ten matches. The club's longest period without a competitive honour is 17 years, between the [1953-54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953%E2%80%9354_in_English_football) and [1969–70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_in_English_football) seasons. [Ted Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Drake) holds the record for most competitive goals in a single season for Arsenal; he scored 44 during the [1934–35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934%E2%80%9335_in_English_football) campaign. ## History

When Arsenal was founded in 1886 by [munition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munition) workers' from Woolwich, the club resisted the lure of professionalism and remained an amateur side. Success in local cup competitions soon followed, and a tie against [Derby County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.) in the FA Cup on 17 January 1891 led to the opposition approaching two of Arsenal's players, in view of offering them professional contracts. Later that year the club resigned its membership of the [Kent County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_County_Football_Association) and [London Football Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Football_Association)– both amateur governing bodies – and voted to turn professional, a move which attracted criticism from many southern clubs. In 1893, the club received an invitation to join [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League), which the board accepted. Arsenal played in the Second Division for eleven seasons, while also participating in regional competitions, the [Southern Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Combination_Football_League) and [United League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_League_(football)). The club won promotion in 1904, and enjoyed strong FA Cup campaigns in the mid-1900s, but the increase of football clubs in the capital and falling attendances at the [Manor Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Ground_(Plumstead)) pushed Arsenal close to bankruptcy by 1910. Sir [Henry Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_George_Norris) and William Hall in that year took over Arsenal, and planned to relocate the team to Highbury in order to improve their financial standing. Arsenal were relegated back to the Second Division in 1913, but the move to North London brought about larger attendances than ever before.

A valedictory campaign was held at [Highbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_Stadium) during [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_in_English_football), to mark Arsenal's final season at the ground.

In 1919, Norris arranged for the club's promotion back to the First Division, in contentious circumstances. With increased financial resources, the club established themselves as a permanent fixture in the division and was better able to spend money on new players. In [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FA_Cup_Final), Arsenal beat [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_A.F.C.) to win its first major piece of silverware: the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup). Success continued right throughout the decade, as they won five league championships and a further FA Cup in seven years. Following the Second World War, Arsenal won two more championships and a FA Cup, but their fortunes gradually declined. It was not until 1970 that the club won another trophy – the [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup), a European club competition designed to promote [trade fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_fairs). A first [league and cup double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_(association_football)#England) was completed a year later; by the end of the decade Arsenal added another FA Cup, beating [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) in the [1979 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FA_Cup_Final). 1980 saw Arsenal lose two finals in quick succession, defeated by [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.) in the [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_FA_Cup_Final) and then to [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_C.F.) in the [Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_Final) on [penalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football)). The club won their first [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) in 1987, but a year later failed to retain the trophy as outsiders [Luton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Town_F.C.) beat them in the final. In 1989, Arsenal won their first league championship in 18 years, courtesy of [Michael Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Thomas_(footballer,_born_1967))' last-minute goal against closest challengers Liverpool in [the final game of the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C._0%E2%80%932_Arsenal_F.C._(26_May_1989)). The club did not build on their success, finishing fourth [the following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Football_League), but regained the title in 1991. As champions, Arsenal were eligible to play in the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League), but their time in the competition ended abruptly as they were eliminated in the second round by [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica).

The growth of commercialism in English football during the late 1980s and early 1990s paved the way for Arsenal and other prominent clubs to seek the possibility of setting up a new top-flight division. Unhappy with how income was distributed to the lower leagues and wanting to exploit television rights, Arsenal and 21 other First Division clubs handed a notice of resignation from the Football League by August 1991. The breakaway division, entitled the Premier League, was administered by [The Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) and received financial backing from [Sky Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_UK). Arsenal finished 10th in the [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_FA_Premier_League); the club did well in other competitions, winning a unique FA and League Cup double. They were victorious in the [1994 Cup Winners' Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_Final), and came close to defending the trophy in 1995, before losing to [Real Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Zaragoza).

Arsenal added more league and cup doubles in 1998 and 2002, and in 2004 became the first club in Premier League history to win the title without a single defeat. The side, nicknamed " [The Invincibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Invincibles_(football))" remained unbeaten for 49 games, before losing to Manchester United in October 2004. In 2006, Arsenal reached their first [UEFA Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_UEFA_Champions_League_Final) but [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona) scored twice in the second half to win the competition. Later that year, Arsenal moved to the [Emirates Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium) which commenced a transitional period. Though the club strengthened their position in the league's "top four" and frequently participated in the Champions League, they struggled to hold on to their best players. In [2011–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_in_English_football), Arsenal made their worst start to a season for 58 years, but a strong finish saw the club overtake [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_London_derby) Tottenham Hotspur to third position. After nine years without silverware, the club beat [Hull City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_City_F.C.) to win the [2014 FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FA_Cup_Final) and [retained the trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FA_Cup_Final) with a dominant display against [Aston Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Villa_F.C.) in 2015, before clinching a record 13th in 2017. Arsenal won their fourteenth FA Cup in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_FA_Cup_Final), beating [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.).

1. Joint holder of the European Golden Shoe with [Diego Forlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Forl%C3%A1n), both of whom scored 25 goals.

5. Arsenal were deducted two points for their part in [a brawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.%E2%80%93Arsenal_F.C._brawl_(1990)) with [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) on 20 October 1990.
6. This season saw the introduction of [three points for a win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win) instead of two.
7. Arsenal did not participate in the [1971 FA Charity Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_FA_Charity_Shield) due to a previously arranged pre-season tour that clashed with the event. [Leicester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.) were invited to take part, and beat Cup runners-up [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.).
8. For the first six seasons of the [Football League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), Arsenal declined to take part as its board preferred the London-centric Floodlit Cup (which became defunct once the new competition was introduced), and were against the League Cup acting as "an interim step towards league re-organisation." Arsenal eventually joined in the [1966–67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966%E2%80%9367_in_English_football) season for reasons unspecified; by the [1971–72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_in_English_football) season it became mandatory for all Football League clubs to participate.
9. Arsenal were not invited to take part in the [1950 FA Charity Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FA_Charity_Shield) despite winning the FA Cup, as the match was held between the [England national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_football_team) that competed in the [1950 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) and the FA squad that participated in an exhibition tour of Canada during the same summer.
10. The FA Cup resumed in 1945, but league football remained regionally structured until the start of the 1946–47 season.
11. In September 1939 first-class football was suspended due to the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), with the 1939–40 league table voided and regional competitions contested instead.
12. No competitive football was played between 1915 and 1919 due to the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). Arsenal competed in the London Combination from 1915–16 to 1918–19.
13. Arsenal were originally awarded sixth place due to an error calculating [goal average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_average), which was not corrected until 1975.
14. Although they had finished outside the automatic promotion places in 1914–15, Arsenal were re-elected to the First Division after it expanded in 1919. According to [Bernard Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Joy), Arsenal secured eighteen votes to [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.)'s eight. However it has been alleged that Arsenal's promotion, on historical grounds rather than merit, was thanks to underhand actions by Norris, who was chairman of the club at the time; see [History of Arsenal F.C. (1886–1966)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Arsenal_F.C._(1886%E2%80%931966)) for further details. These allegations range from political machinations to outright bribery; no firm proof of any wrongdoing has ever been found.
15. Arsenal finished with exactly the same league record as [Blackburn Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.).
16. The club was a founding member of the United League in 1896. The league lasted for three seasons before it disbanded.
17. Includes goals scored in the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League), [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League), [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) and [Charity/Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield). London Challenge Cup results are sourced to Kelly's first team line-ups (until the 1929–30 season) and Ollier (1995) thereafter. All other results, including the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup which was not administered by UEFA, are sourced to Kelly.
19. The expansion of the FA Cup, from 15 clubs in its [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871%E2%80%9372_FA_Cup) to currently more than 700 has meant successive changes in the competition's structure. Teams in the top two divisions were made exempt from the qualifying stages and the first two rounds of the Cup in [1925–26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925%E2%80%9326_FA_Cup). Because of this rounds were renamed; the fifth and sixth qualifying rounds became first and second round proper. Since [1914–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914%E2%80%9315_FA_Cup) there have been a total of 14 rounds, in comparison to five in [1871–72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871%E2%80%9372_FA_Cup).
20. Despite their status as champions in 1989, Arsenal were barred from participating in the [1989–90 European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_European_Cup) as [UEFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA)'s ban on English teams from playing in European club competitions was in effect. The embargo which began in 1985 as a consequence of Liverpool's role in the [Heysel tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Stadium_disaster), was not lifted until April 1990.
21. Contrary to what was stated at the time, clubs from Southern England did not boycott Arsenal following their move to professional football, nor were the club expelled from the London or Kent FA.
22. A club located in the southern [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties) of England. Initially these were amateur clubs, as professionalism in football was not as readily accepted in the south as in the north. In the 1893–94 season, Arsenal (under its former name Woolwich Arsenal) turned professional and became the first southern club admitted to the northern-oriented [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League). The following year saw the creation of the [Southern Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League), which was composed of amateur and professional teams. By the [1920–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920%E2%80%9321_in_English_football) season, the top division of the Southern Football League was absorbed by the Football League, to create its third division.
