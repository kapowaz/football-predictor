List of Peterborough United F.C. seasons

[Peterborough United Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_United_Football_Club) is an English [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough), [Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire). After former [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League) club [Peterborough & Fletton United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_&amp;_Fletton_United_F.C.) folded in 1932, there had been no senior football in the Peterborough area. In the summer of 1934, a new professional club, named Peterborough United, was founded to take its place. It was welcomed into membership of the [Midland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Football_League_(1889)) for 1934–35, and the club's first team finished that initial season in mid-table. They entered the national [cup competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_competition), the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup), the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935%E2%80%9336_FA_Cup), but lost their opening match 3–0 at home to [Rushden Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushden_Town_F.C.) in the first qualifying round. When competitive football resumed after the Second World War, Peterborough enjoyed improved performances in both league and cup competition. In the cup, they regularly reached the rounds proper, and progressed to the fourth round in 1956–57 and 1959–60, eliminating two [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) teams on the first occasion and three on the second. In the Midland League, they finished second in 1953–54, third the following year, and then embarked on a run of five consecutive championships, scoring more than 100 goals in each campaign. Repeated attempts at election to the Football League failed – albeit narrowly in 1958–59 – until they finally gained admission to the [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) in 1960 at the expense of [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead_A.F.C.).

Their momentum continued into the new Football League season: in second place with Christmas approaching, Peterborough did not lose again until the following April. They won the Fourth Division title, scoring what remains a Football League record 134 goals. [Terry Bly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Bly) supplied 52 of those 134 – not a league record, but a seasonal total which has not been surpassed since. They followed up with four top-half finishes at the higher level before dropping to mid-table, eliminated [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) among others on their way to the sixth round (quarter-final) of the [1964–65 FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964%E2%80%9365_FA_Cup), and went one step further in the [1965–66 League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_Football_League_Cup), losing to [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion) in the semifinal, but problems off the field disrupted their further progress. In November 1967, [the Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) and Football League met to consider charges of making illegal payments to players, poor accounting practices and poor internal governance, stemming from claims surrounding an FA Cup match against [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) the previous January. They decided that Peterborough would be demoted to the Fourth Division at the end of the [1967–68 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_Football_League). At the time, they stood fourth in the [Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) after 19 matches. They won their second Fourth Division championship in [1973–74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Football_League), this time spending five years in the Third before returning to the fourth tier until 1991.

Back-to-back promotions – via a fourth-place finish in 1990–91 and the [play-offs in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Football_League_play-offs#Third_Division), beating [Stockport County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport_County) 2–1 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Football_League_Third_Division_play-off_final), – earned Peterborough a place in the second tier for the first time in the club's history. They came 10th in what was called the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Football_League#First_Division)– when the newly formed [FA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Premier_League) split from the Football League, the remaining divisions of the Football League were renumbered upwards – which remains the team's highest league finish. and were relegated in 1993–94. They returned to the second tier, which by then had been rebranded as the [Football League Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Championship), in 2009, again after two consecutive promotions. Although relegated straight back to [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_One), they were immediately re-promoted, and stayed up for two seasons. Failure in the [2014 play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Football_League_play-offs) was offset by a first ever victory in a nationally organised cup competition: Peterborough defeated [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield_F.C.) by three goals to one in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Football_League_Trophy_Final) of the [2013–14 Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Football_League_Trophy), a competition open to clubs in the third and fourth tiers of English football. They returned to the Championship as [2020–21 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_EFL_League_One) runners-up, went straight back down, and lost to [Sheffield Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday) on penalties in the [2023 play-off semifinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_EFL_play-offs). They won the [2023–24 EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_EFL_Trophy), beating [Wycombe Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycombe_Wanderers) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_EFL_Trophy_final), but again lost in the play-offs.

As of the end of the 2023–24 season, the team have spent 25 seasons in the fourth tier of the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), 33 in the third, and 6 in the second. ## Notes

1. Peterborough lost 2–1 on aggregate to [Oxford United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_United) in the [play-off semifinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_English_Football_League_play-offs#League_One).
2. Clarke-Harris was joint top scorer in the [2022–23 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_EFL_League_One) season with 26 goals.
3. Peterborough won the first (home) leg of the [play-off semifinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_English_Football_League_play-offs#League_One) against [Sheffield Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday) 4–0, but conceded four goals, including a 98th-minute equaliser, in the second leg. They regained the lead in extra time via an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal_(association_football)), but conceded again to take the tie to penalties. Wednesday won the [shoot-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football)) 5–3.
4. Clarke-Harris was top scorer in the [2020–21 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_EFL_League_One) season with 31 goals.
5. Toney was top scorer in the [2019–20 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_EFL_League_One) season with 24 goals.
6. The 2019–20 football season was disrupted by the effects of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom). The EFL was suspended in March 2020 and in June, the League One clubs voted to end the regular season programme early. Teams had not all played the same number of matches, so it was agreed to construct final league tables on a points-per-game basis. Peterborough United's 74.17 points per game placed them 7th.
7. Marriott was top scorer in the [2017–18 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_EFL_League_One) season with 27 goals.
8. Lost 3–2 on aggregate to [Leyton Orient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyton_Orient) in the [semifinal of the play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Football_League_play-offs#League_One).
9. Mackail-Smith was top scorer in the [2010–11 League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Football_League_One) season with 27 goals.
10. After defeating [Milton Keynes Dons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Keynes_Dons) 4–3 on aggregate in the [semifinal of the play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Football_League_play-offs#League_One), Peterborough beat [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_A.F.C.) 3–0 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Football_League_One_play-off_final).
11. From the 2004–05 season, the Football League divisions were rebranded: Division One was renamed the [Football League Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Championship), and Divisions Two and Three became [Football League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_One) and [Football League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Two) respectively.
12. After defeating [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnet_F.C.) 5–1 on aggregate in the [semifinal of the play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Football_League_play-offs#Third_Division), Peterborough beat [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington_F.C.) 2–1 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Football_League_Third_Division_play-off_final).
13. Martindale was top scorer in the [1995–96 Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Football_League#Second_Division) with 21 goals, of which 6 were scored for [Notts County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notts_County_F.C.) after he left Peterborough.
14. Peterborough's record high finish, of 10th in the second tier.
15. The newly formed [FA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Premier_League) split from the Football League, and the remaining divisions of the Football League were renumbered upwards.
16. Promoted to the second tier for the first time. After defeating [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_A.F.C.) 4–3 [on aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie) in the [semifinal of the play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Football_League_play-offs#Third_Division), Peterborough beat [Stockport County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport_County) 2–1 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Football_League_Third_Division_play-off_final).
17. Figure includes three goals in the [Football League Group Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Group_Cup), regarded as competitive by Up The Posh! and by independent sources such as the English National Football Archive (ENFA) but omitted from Cooke's competitive record by the Peterborough United Football Club database.
18. The 1981–82 season saw the introduction of [three points for a win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win) instead of two.
19. Price was top scorer in the [1971–72 Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Football_League#Fourth_Division) with 28 goals.
20. Demoted for financial irregularities.
21. Bly was top scorer in the [1960–61 Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%9361_Football_League#Fourth_Division) with 52 goals.
22. In their first season in [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League), Peterborough not only won the Fourth Division title, they set a League record of 134 goals which still stands, and [Terry Bly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Bly)'s 52-goal return, while not a League record, has not been bettered since.
23. The FA Cup was contested in [1945–46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945%E2%80%9346_FA_Cup). From the first round proper to the sixth round (quarter-final), results were determined on [aggregate score over two legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie).
24. The 1939–40 Midland League season was abandoned with three matches played when the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) began, and competitive football [remained suspended for the duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_during_World_War_II).
25. From the [1960–61 Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%9361_Football_League) season onwards, divisions are sorted according to their level within the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), and separately from the pre-Football League division.

Includes goals scored in all nationally organised competitions, i.e. the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_League), including [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_play-offs), [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup), [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), [Football League Group Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Group_Cup), [EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy) and predecessors, and [Anglo-Italian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Italian_Cup), as well as goals scored in the [Midland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Football_League_(1889)) and [London Midweek League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Midweek_League?action=edit&amp;redlink=1) before Peterborough's admission to the Football League. For seasons from 1934–35 to 2009–10, sourced to the Peterborough United Football Club database; from 2010–11 onwards sourced to Soccerbase.
