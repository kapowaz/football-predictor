List of Southampton F.C. seasons

[Southampton Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_F.C.), an English [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton), [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire), was founded in [1885](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1885%E2%80%9386_St._Mary's_Y.M.A._season) as St. Mary's Y.M.A. For almost six years the club took no part in any official competitions at national level, playing only friendlies and local tournaments, including the [Hampshire Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_Senior_Cup). St Mary's first entered the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) in the [1891–92 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1891%E2%80%9392_in_English_football), and in 1894 under the name Southampton St Mary's joined the newly founded [Southern Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League).

Southampton league performance from 1920 to 2024

The club changed its name to simply Southampton at the start of the [1896–97 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896%E2%80%9397_in_English_football), and quickly established themselves as the primary force in football in the South of England, winning the Southern League three times in a row. The club also reached the semi-finals of the FA Cup in 1898, losing the replay 2–0 to [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) after a 1–1 draw. The Saints continued to dominate the league into the 20th century, claiming the championship again in 1901, 1903 and 1904, before joining the [Football League Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) as a founding member in 1920, following the absorption of the Southern League. Southampton were almost instantly promoted to the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division), completing the feat as champions in the [1921–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921%E2%80%9322_in_English_football).

Southampton found life in the second flight of English football difficult, as they remained a mid-bottom table side throughout the seasons played before and immediately after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), before they were ultimately relegated back to the Third Division in 1953. Promoted as champions again in 1960, Southampton performed well in the following ten years, reaching the quarter-final stage in the newly created [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) in 1961 and earning promotion to [Division One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) in 1966 as division runners-up. The club received its first taste of continental football in the [1969–70 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_in_English_football) when they competed in the [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup), although were knocked out in the first round. Despite suffering relegation back to Division Two in 1974, Southampton achieved a surprise victory in the [1976 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_FA_Cup_final) against [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) to win the trophy for the first and only time in the club's history. This success was topped off two seasons later when the club regained its First Division place.

The [1983–84 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_in_English_football) was Southampton's most successful in terms of league position, when the club finished second in the top flight to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), missing out on the championship by just three points. In the 1990s, the club largely struggled to get out of the bottom third of the table, although they reached the advanced stages of the FA Cup and League Cup on a number of occasions and also played in the [Full Members Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Members_Cup) final in 1992. The club became founder members of the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) in 1992 but continued their disappointing performances, despite reaching the [2003 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FA_Cup_final) against [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) and losing to a single goal. Southampton were relegated to the [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) in the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_in_English_football), and were relegated again following off-the-pitch problems in 2009. However, Southampton were galvanised when [Marcus Liebherr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Liebherr) rescued them from administration on 8 July 2009, winning the [Johnstone's Paint Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy) with an emphatic 4–1 win against [Carlisle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_United_F.C.). The following season, Southampton were promoted to the Championship as runners-up in League One and finished runners-up again the next season in the Championship to return to the Premier League. The club subsequently enjoyed four consecutive top eight finishes in the top flight, qualifying for the [Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_League) twice, as well as reaching the [2017 EFL Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_EFL_Cup_final) where they were beaten 3–2 by Manchester United. Southampton would then be relegated in 2023, before gaining promotion straight back to the Premier League via the playoffs the following season.

As of the end of the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_in_English_football), the club have spent 47 seasons in the top division of English football, 40 in the second, and 11 in the third.
