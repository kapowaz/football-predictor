[Watford Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_Football_Club) is an English [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club from [Watford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford), [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire). Formed on 15 April 1898 as a result of the amalgamation of two strong local clubs, Watford St. Mary's and West Herts. West Herts began life as Watford Rovers in 1881, the club entered the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) for the first time in 1886. In the same year, they also entered the county-wide Herts Senior Cup, reaching the final six times over the next ten years. Watford Rovers became West Herts in 1891, and joined the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League) for the 1896–97 season. The team started to change from one composed entirely of amateurs to one including paid professionals. In 1898, West Herts amalgamated with Watford St Mary's to form a new club, Watford Football Club.

The club participated in the Southern League from 1896 until 1920, experiencing considerable success. They won six league titles in this period, including the Southern League First Division in [1914–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914%E2%80%9315_Watford_F.C._season). After the resumption of Southern League football following a four-year hiatus due to the First World War, Watford missed out on a second consecutive title in [1919–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919%E2%80%9320_Watford_F.C._season) on [goal average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_average). They joined the [Football League Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) in the 1920–21 season, and following its subsequent reorganisation became founder members of the [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South) in 1921.

Watford competed in the Third Division South for the next 37 years, with little success. [Fred Pagnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Pagnam) finished as the Division's top scorer in 1922–23, the club reached the final of the [Third Division South Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South_Cup) in 1935 and 1937 (winning on the latter occasion), and [Len Dunderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Dunderdale) scored 21 goals for Watford in 1938–39 despite leaving midway through the season. The team started to progress after the reorganisation of the Football League into four national divisions in 1958. They won promotion to the Third Division in 1960, the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) in 1969, and reached the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) semi-final in 1970. However, the league progress was reversed with two relegations over the next five years, and in 1976–77 Watford were briefly bottom of the entire Football League.

Watford Performances from 1920 until 2023

A turning point in the club's history came in the late 1970s. Singer, shareholder and lifelong Watford supporter [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John) became chairman in 1976, and appointed [Graham Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Taylor_(footballer)) as manager in 1977. The club achieved consecutive promotions between 1977 and 1979, and reached the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) for the first time in their history in 1982. Furthermore, in 1982–83 Watford finished second in the First Division, and [Luther Blissett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_Blissett) was the division's top scorer with 27 goals. Consequently, Watford qualified for the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup) in 1983–84. They also reached their first [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_FA_Cup_Final), losing 2–0 to [Everton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F.C.) at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_(1923)). Following Taylor's departure in 1987, Watford were relegated in 1988.

Watford remained in English football's second tier for eight seasons, until they were relegated in 1995–96. Taylor returned as manager in 1997, and for the second time in his career led Watford to consecutive promotions, although he was unable to prevent relegation from the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) in 1999–2000. In the 21st century Watford have reached three FA Cup semi-finals, one [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) semi-final, and spent three further season in the Premier League after winning the [2006 Football League Championship play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Football_League_Championship_play-off_final) under the management of [Aidy Boothroyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidy_Boothroyd) and following automatic promotion under [Slaviša Jokanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavi%C5%A1a_Jokanovi%C4%87) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Watford_F.C._season).

### Early history

The club was formed on 15 April 1898 as a result of the amalgamation of two strong local clubs, Watford St. Mary's and West Herts. West Herts began life as Watford Rovers in 1881, when George Devereux de Vere Capell, [Earl of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Essex) and owner of [Cassiobury Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiobury_Park), gave a group of boys permission to use the grounds for football. However, the agreement stipulated that the team could not play organised competitive matches on the estate. Members of the group included [Henry Grover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Grover_(footballer)?action=edit&amp;redlink=1), the man later recognised as the club's founder, and [Charlie Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Peacock_(footballer)?action=edit&amp;redlink=1), who played for [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire_County_Football_Association), became involved with board meetings at the club, and became the proprietor of the [Watford Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_Observer), the local newspaper. Over the next five years the team participated exclusively in [friendly matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendly_match) against schools and local clubs. Matches played when the club was not entering competitions included the first recorded fixture against future [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Town_F.C._and_Watford_F.C._rivalry) [Luton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Town); a 1–0 home win to Watford on 5 December 1885. In the 1886–87 season, Watford Rovers entered the FA Cup for the first time, although they were eliminated in their opening game. They have competed in at least one competition in every season since. From 1886 Rovers participated in the Herts Senior Cup, a competition open to all clubs in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire), winning it on four occasions. They also participated in the Hennessey Cup—open to clubs within a 10-mile (16 km) radius of [Uxbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uxbridge)—between 1888 and 1891. Rovers' first match against Watford St Mary's was a 7–4 home win on 17 January 1891. The teams met on eleven further occasions prior to their amalgamation in 1898. In total, Rovers and their successors West Herts won six times, St Mary's four times, and the remaining two matches ended in draws.

### League history

From 1896–97 until 1919–20, the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) and [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League) ran in parallel, and were organised by separate bodies. The Southern League was therefore not part of the English football league pyramid. In 1920–21, the Southern League First Division was absorbed by the Football League, thus becoming part of the [English football pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), initially as the Football League Third Division. From 1921–22 until 1957–58, the [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South) operated as the third highest level of English football, running in parallel with the [Third Division North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North). From 1958–59 until 1991–92, Division 1 was the highest level of English football, Division 2 the second highest, Division 3 the third highest and Division 4 the fourth highest. The Premier League was formed in 1992–93, and since then has been the highest level of English football. Division 1 became the second level, and Division 2 the third level. In 2004–05, Division 1 was renamed as the Football League Championship.

27. Divisions are sorted according to their level within the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system) at the time.
28. Seasons up to 1997–98 sourced to Watford Season by Season. Seasons from 1998–99 onwards sourced to Statto.com.
29. The semi-final of the Senior Cup was abandoned at 2–2, owing to crowd trouble after a St Albans goal was disallowed. Watford Rovers declined to contest the replay, in order "to prevent further ill-feeling."
30. The United League was contested by clubs from southern and eastern England, including [Brighton and Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove_Albion), [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C.), [Luton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_Town_F.C.), [Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) and [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_F.C.).
31. The Bucks & Contiguous Counties League was, as the name suggests, open to clubs in the vicinity of the county of [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire). Other participants in the league included [Wycombe Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycombe_Wanderers), [Chesham Generals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesham_Generals), [Aylesbury United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aylesbury_United) and [Hitchin Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchin_Town).
