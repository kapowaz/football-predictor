[Lincoln City Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_City_Football_Club), an English [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Lincoln, Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Lincolnshire), was founded in 1884. The club's first team won the major regional trophy, the [Lincolnshire Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire_Senior_Cup), in the [1886–87 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886%E2%80%9387_in_English_football), and reached the last 16 of the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) in the same year. In 1888, the club joined [the Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination), a league set up to provide organised football for those clubs not invited to join [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) which was to start the same year. However, the Combination was not well organised and folded in April 1889 with many fixtures still outstanding. Lincoln then became founder members of the [Midland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Football_League_(1889)), and won the inaugural league title. After two seasons the club turned professional and joined the [Football Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Alliance); the following year they were elected to the newly formed [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) of the Football League. Their highest finishing position – fifth in the Second Division – was achieved in [1901–02](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1901%E2%80%9302_Football_League), and in the same season they reached the last 16 of the FA Cup for the third time. Lincoln failed to gain [re-election to the League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-election_(Football_League)) three times between 1909 and 1920; on each occasion, they won the championship of the league to which they had been demoted, either the Midland League or, in 1912, [the Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League_(England)), and made an immediate return to the Football League.

In 1921, Lincoln were founder members of the [Football League Third Division North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North), and ten seasons later won the division title, thus gaining promotion to the Second Division, though for one season only. In 1947–48, Lincoln again won the Third Division North title, and again suffered immediate relegation from the Second. On regaining Second Division status three years later they remained in the division until the 1960–61 season, but then suffered consecutive relegations. The club website rates [1975–76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_in_English_football) as "by far the most successful season in the club's history". They won the [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) title with a record points total for any division before the introduction of [three points for a win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win), set new Fourth Division records for most wins and fewest defeats, and were undefeated at home, with 21 wins and 2 draws.

For many years, teams finishing at the bottom of the Football League had to apply for re-election to the League for the following season – Lincoln made eleven successful applications – but in the 1986–87 season, automatic promotion and relegation was introduced between the [Football Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Conference), the fifth tier of English football, and the Football League Fourth Division. Lincoln finished bottom of the [1986–87 Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Football_League_Fourth_Division) and thus became the first club automatically relegated from the League. They made an immediate return as Conference champions, and until 2011 spent all but one season in the bottom League division. Lincoln reached the promotion play-offs in five consecutive seasons, between 2003 and 2007, but were unsuccessful on each occasion. The 2006–07 season marked Lincoln's 100th season in the Football League; they were the first club to reach that milestone without ever playing in the top division of the League. After a return of only two points from Lincoln's last 11 games of the 2010–11 season allowed [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnet_F.C.) to overturn an 11-point deficit, they returned to non-League football. Five successive bottom-half finishes in the Conference (renamed the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_(division)) for 2015–16) preceded Lincoln's return to the Football League as [champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_National_League). They combined it with an FA Cup run in which they eliminated three Football League teams, including [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_EFL_Championship) runners-up [Brighton & Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_&amp;_Hove_Albion), before a [1–0 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_0%E2%80%931_Lincoln_City_(2017)) away to [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) club [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C.) made them the first non-league club since [1913–14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913%E2%80%9314_FA_Cup) to reach the quarter-finals; in the quarter-final, they lost 5–0 away to [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.). In their first season back, they made their first competitive trip to [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium), where they beat [Shrewsbury Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_Town_F.C.) 1–0 to win the [EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_EFL_Trophy), and reached the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_English_Football_League_play-offs#League_Two), in which they lost to [Exeter City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_City_F.C.). They achieved promotion [the following season as champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_League_Two), and two years later reached but lost in the [play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_EFL_League_One_play-off_final).

Since their election to the Football League in 1892, Lincoln have spent 36 seasons in the second tier, 35 in the third, 40 in the fourth, and 10 seasons in [non-League football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-League_football). ## Notes

1. After beating [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) 3–2 on aggregate in the [play-off semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_English_Football_League_play-offs#League_One), lost 2–1 to [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool_F.C.) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_EFL_League_One_play-off_final).
2. The 2019–20 football season was disrupted by the effects of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom). The EFL was suspended in March 2020 and in June, the League One clubs voted to end the regular season programme early. Teams had not all played the same number of matches, so it was agreed to construct final league tables on a points-per-game basis. Lincoln City's 52.80 points per game placed them 16th.
3. Beat [Shrewsbury Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_Town_F.C.) 1–0 in the [2018 EFL Trophy Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_EFL_Trophy_Final) at [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium).
4. Lost to [Exeter City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_City_F.C.) 3–1 on aggregate in the [play-off semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_English_Football_League_play-offs#League_Two).
5. The Football Conference was renamed the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_(English_football)).
6. Reached the play-offs for the fifth successive season: lost 7–4 on aggregate to [Bristol Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Rovers) in the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Two_play-offs#2007).
7. Lost 3–1 to [Grimsby Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Town) on aggregate in the [play-off semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Two_play-offs#2006).
8. Beat [Macclesfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macclesfield_Town) 2–1 on aggregate in the semi-final before losing 2–0 to [Southend United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southend_United) after [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_time) in the [2005 Football League Two play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Football_League_Two_play-off_final).
9. Division 3 was renamed League 2 as part of a rebranding exercise by the Football League.
10. Lost 4–3 to [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town) on aggregate in the [play-off semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Two_play-offs#2004).
11. Beat [Scunthorpe United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scunthorpe_United) 6–3 on aggregate in the semi-final before losing 5–2 to [AFC Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Bournemouth) in the [2003 Football League Third Division play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Football_League_Third_Division_play-off_final).
12. When the newly formed [FA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Premier_League) split from the Football League, the remaining divisions of the Football League were renumbered upwards.
13. Lincoln became the first team to suffer automatic relegation to the [Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Conference).
14. Competed in the [Football League Group Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Group_Cup), a successor to the [Anglo-Scottish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Scottish_Cup), failing to progress past the group stage in the first season, but reaching the final in the second, losing 3–2 at home to [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.). The competition was renamed the Football League Trophy for that second season, but is often referred to as the [Football League Group Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Group_Trophy) to distinguish from the more recent [Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Trophy), of which it was a forerunner.
15. The 1981–82 season saw the introduction of [three points for a win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win) instead of two.
16. Set an all-time record attendance of 23,196 against [Derby County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.) in the League Cup.
17. Set a record FA Cup attendance of 23,027 against [Preston North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_North_End).
18. Scored 36 goals in the Third Division North.
19. Beat [Crewe Alexandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewe_Alexandra) 11–1 at home to set a record league victory.
20. Set a record league attendance of 23,146 against [Grimsby Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Town).
21. Scored 32 goals in the Third Division North.
22. Although the Football League did not resume until the 1946–47 season, the FA Cup was contested in 1945–46. From the first round proper to the sixth round (quarter-final), results were determined on aggregate score [over two legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie).
23. The [1939–40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939%E2%80%9340_in_English_football) season was abandoned with three matches played when the Second World War began. Lincoln City do not include appearances and goals from those three matches in players' career records.
24. Lincoln were one of eight clubs who chose not to enter the Third Division North Cup.
25. Scored 42 goals in the Third Division North.
26. After finishing next to bottom of the League, Lincoln finished fourth in the ballot for election to the two places available. Although the League expanded by forming a Third Division for the following season, the new division was made up of Southern clubs only, mostly from the First Division of the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League). In 1921–22, a Third Division North was formed to run in parallel with the Southern section.
27. After finishing bottom of the League, Lincoln failed to be re-elected, so joined the newly formed [Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League_(England)), while their [reserve team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_team) retained membership of the [Midland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Football_League_(1889)).
28. Lincoln finished bottom of the League and failed to be re-elected. After [Stoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C.) later resigned from the League, a further election was held in which Lincoln and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur) twice polled an equal number of votes. "The matter was then left in the hands of the Management Committee, who decided in favour of Tottenham Hotspur."
29. Divisions are sorted according to their level within the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system) at the time.
30. Goals scored in the following first-team competitions are counted: [the Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination), [Midland League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Football_League_(1889)), [Football Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Alliance), [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League), including play-offs, [the Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League_(England)), [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_(English_football)) and predecessors, [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup), [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), [EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy) and predecessors, [Third Division North Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North_Cup), [Watney Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watney_Cup), [FA Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Trophy) and [Conference League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_League_Cup). For seasons up to and including 2012–13, sourced to the Lincoln City FC Archive; from 2013–14 to 2016–17, to Soccerway; from 2017–18 onwards, to Soccerbase. Select season required via dropdown menu.
31. The [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) competition started in the [1960–61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%9361_in_English_football) season.
32. Beginning with the 1925–26 season, the FA Cup was structured so that the third round proper contained 64 teams. Prior to that date, the structure had varied, so rounds are not directly comparable to the round of the same name after 1925. For example, in 1892–93, Lincoln's first season in the Football League, there were only three rounds proper before the semifinal, as compared with the current six.
33. An attempt was made to set up a league called [The Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination) involving clubs not invited to join [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League). Lack of proper organisation meant it was wound up in April 1889 with many fixtures still outstanding. Lincoln City played 14 of their full 16 fixtures.
