List of Cardiff City F.C. seasons

[Cardiff City Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_City_F.C.), a professional [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales), was founded in 1899 as Riverside A.F.C. by members of Riverside Cricket Club. The club's first year was made up of [friendlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_game) before they were admitted to the [Cardiff & District League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_&amp;_District_League) in 1900 and later the [South Wales League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales_League) in 1907. The following year, the club were granted permission to adopt the name Cardiff City, having been denied the previous year as they were deemed not to be playing at a high enough level, and moved into the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system) for the first time by joining Division Two of the [Southern Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League). They won promotion to Division One in the 1912–13 season and remained there until 1920, playing just three seasons during this time due to the outbreak of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) when league football was abandoned. In 1920, they were elected to [The Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League), joining the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) for one season, winning promotion in their first season after finishing second. The club also competed regularly in the [Welsh Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Cup), winning the trophy 22 times during their history, second only to [Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham_A.F.C.)'s 23, before they were denied entrance into the competition from 1995 onward by the [Football Association of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Wales), along with all other clubs playing in the [English league pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system).

The following decade is regarded as the most successful in the club's history as they finished second during the 1923–24 season, losing out on winning the league title on the goal average system used at the time to [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_F.C.). During this period, they reached two [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) finals, losing 1–0 to [Sheffield United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.) in [1925](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925_FA_Cup_Final) before returning to the final two years later, beating [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) 1–0 at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_(1923)) in [1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927_FA_Cup_Final). They also won the [FA Charity Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Charity_Shield) after beating amateur side [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthians_F.C.) [2–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927_FA_Charity_Shield). Cardiff remain the only non-English side to have ever won the FA Cup or FA Charity Shield.

Cardiff City's league positions between 1920 and 2023

Cardiff were relegated for the first time in their history in 1929 and entered a period of decline, dropping into the third tier two years later. The club did manage to return to the top tier in 1952, but their relegation in 1962 led to a decline in the club's fortunes that saw them outside the top division for 51 years, the longest absence in the club's history, not returning until 2013. Relegation from Division Two in the 1984–85 season saw the club enter a downward period that culminated with their joint lowest ever finish in the Football League, 22nd in the fourth tier in the 1995–96 season.

In 2000, the club was purchased by [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon) businessman [Sam Hammam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hammam), who invested money into the side and saw them rise from the fourth tier to the second tier in just three seasons. Although Hammam left the club in 2006, they continued their progress, reaching the Football League Championship play-offs for three consecutive seasons between 2009 and 2012, suffering defeat on all three occasions. The following season, under the ownership of new owner [Vincent Tan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Tan), they finished first in the Championship, winning promotion back into the top tier for the first time since 1962. However, they suffered relegation in their only season and returned to the Championship.

As of the end of the [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Cardiff_City_F.C._season) season, the club had spent 17 seasons in the top tier of English football, 51 in the second, 20 in the third and 10 in the fourth. This list details their achievements in first-team competitions, and records their top goalscorer, for each completed season since their first appearance in the English football pyramid as members of the Southern Football League in 1910–11. Due to the unavailability of complete statistics, seasons prior to 1910 in the amateur Welsh leagues are not included.

## Notes

1. All games played behind closed doors due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic)
2. Defeated in the Championship play-off semi-final by [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.).
3. Defeated in the Championship play-off semi-final by [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.).
4. Defeated in the Championship play-off semi-final by [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_F.C.).
5. Scored 20 goals in the Championship.
6. Defeated in the Championship play-off final by [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool_F.C.).
7. Division One was renamed [The Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Championship) from the 2004–05 season.
8. Scored 31 goals in Division Two.
9. Promoted to Division One after beating [Queens Park Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.) 1–0 in the play-off final.
10. Defeated in the Division Two play-off semi-final by [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C.).
11. The Associate Members' Cup was renamed the Football League Trophy from the 1992–93 season.
12. The Fourth Division was renamed Division Three after the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) broke away from the Football League.
13. This season saw the introduction of [three points for a win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win) instead of two.
14. Scored 22 goals in Division Two.
15. The FA Cup competition resumed for the 1945–46 season, however league football did not resume until the following season.
16. The club played 254 competitive games in regional league and cup football between 1939 and 1946. Guest players were permitted, and results and records from this period are not included in official statistics.
17. The 1939–40 Welsh Cup tournament was completed.
18. The Football League season was abandoned in September 1939 after three matches had been played and all results were annulled.
19. The Third Division South Cup semi-final replay and final were postponed until the following season. However, due to the outbreak of Second World War, the competition was abandoned at the semi-final stage.
20. Cardiff City were forced to apply for re-election after finishing bottom of the Third Division South.
21. Cardiff City won the tournament by defeating [Rhyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyl_F.C.) in a replay which was not played until 8 October 1930, during the following season.
22. As winners of the FA Cup during the previous season, Cardiff City entered the FA Charity Shield, defeating amateur side [Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthians_F.C.) 2–1.
23. Cardiff City finished second to [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_F.C.) due to the goal average system used at the time.
24. Cardiff City entered their reserve side in the Welsh Cup for the 1920–21 season.
25. Cardiff were invited to join The Football League in 1920
