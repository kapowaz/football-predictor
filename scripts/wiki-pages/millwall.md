List of Millwall F.C. seasons

[Millwall Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_Rovers) in 1887, the first season they entered the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup).

This is a list of all seasons played by [Millwall Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.) from their early beginnings in the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League), to their inaugural season in the [English Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) and up to their last completed season. It details their record in the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup), the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_Cup) and other major competitions entered, as well as managers, top goalscorers and average home attendance for each season.

Millwall were founded in [1885](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1885%E2%80%9386_Millwall_Rovers_F.C._season) and for the first nine years of their existence did not compete in league football. They first entered the FA Cup in [1887](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896%E2%80%9397_FA_Cup), turning full-time professional as a club in 1893. They were founding members of the Southern League in 1894, which they competed in for 22 seasons, claiming the title twice in [1895](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1894%E2%80%9395_Southern_Football_League) and [1896](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1895%E2%80%9396_Southern_Football_League). They left to join the Football League in the [1920–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920%E2%80%9321_in_English_football). Millwall have played in all four divisions during their 99 consecutive seasons in the league, including the [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South), which they won twice, in [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927%E2%80%9328_in_English_football) and [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937%E2%80%9338_in_English_football). They were [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) champions in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961%E2%80%9362_in_English_football). The [1988–89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Millwall_F.C._season) season stands as The Lions most successful when Millwall reached the top tier of English football, where they finished 10th in their first ever season in the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division), after being promoted as champions of the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_in_English_football). Their lowest league finish is 9th in the founding season of the [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) in [1958–59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958%E2%80%9359_in_English_football). Millwall were Second Division champions in the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Millwall_F.C._season), when they finished with their highest ever points total of 93.

Millwall Performances from 1920 until 2023

Millwall have been promoted eleven times (five as champions) and relegated nine times. Millwall have reached the [Football League play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_play-offs) eight times. They were play-off semi-finalists in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Football_League_play-offs#Second_Division), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Football_League_play-offs#First_Division), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Football_League_play-offs#Second_Division), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Football_League_play-offs#First_Division), finalists in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Football_League_One_play-off_final) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Football_League_One_play-off_final), and they were promoted as winners in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Football_League_One_play-off_final) and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Football_League_One_play-off_final).

They reached the [2004 FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_FA_Cup_Final), losing to [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.), but still qualified for the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_UEFA_Cup#First_round) the following season, playing in Europe for the first time in their history. They have also reached the FA Cup quarter-finals on eleven occasions, and the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_Semi-finals) four times; in [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1899%E2%80%931900_FA_Cup#Semi_finals), [1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903%E2%80%9304_FA_Cup#Semi-finals), [1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9337_FA_Cup#Semi-Finals) and [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FA_Cup). Millwall have reached the quarter-finals of the League Cup on three occasions, in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Football_League_Cup), [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Football_League_Cup) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Football_League_Cup). The Lions won the [Football League Group Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Group_Cup) in 1983 and were runners up in the [Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Trophy) in 1999.

[Teddy Sheringham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Sheringham), [Peter Burridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Burridge), and [Richard Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Parker) hold the record for most goals scored in a season with 38, which they set in the [1990-91 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990-91_in_English_football), [1960–61 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%9361_in_English_football), and [1926–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926%E2%80%9327_in_English_football) respectively. Millwall's highest average home attendance is 27,373, which they set in the [1938–39 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938%E2%80%9339_in_English_football) just before the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) broke out.

## Notes

1. Due to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19) restrictions in the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom), Millwall played 21 of their 23 home games [behind closed doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_closed_doors_(sport)) without fans. They only played two games with fans, and there was a limit of 2,000 allowed to attend.
2. Upon its formation for the [1992–93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_in_English_football) season, the [FA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) became the top tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England); the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division), [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) and [Third Divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) then became the second, third and fourth tiers, respectively.
3. Millwall were placed in the newly formed [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) for the [1958–59 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958%E2%80%9359_in_English_football) due to the Football League restructering and de-regionalisation of [Third Division North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North) and [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South).
4. Millwall's first team manager was director Fred Kidd in 1890, during the first five years of their existence from 1885–1890 the team captain and committee of directors were considered joint managers.
