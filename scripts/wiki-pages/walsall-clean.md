List of Walsall F.C. seasons

The [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall_F.C.) team of 1893

[Walsall Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall_F.C.) was formed in 1888 when Walsall Town and Walsall Swifts amalgamated. They joined [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) in 1892, as founder members of the new [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division).

The table below details the club's achievements in all national competitions and records their average attendance and top goalscorer for each completed league season.

## Notes

1. Lost 0–1 to [AFC Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Wimbledon) in the play-off final.
2. Due to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic all matches were played behind closed doors with no spectators.
3. With an overall PPG of 1.31, Walsall finished in 12th place.
4. The League Two season was delayed on 13 March 2020 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). League Two clubs voted by an overwhelming majority to formally end the 2019–20 season on 9 June 2020, with the final standings to be determined by point-per-game ratios.
5. Lost 1–6 on aggregate to [Barnsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnsley_F.C.) in the play-off semi-final.
6. Walsall reached the final of the [Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy), losing 2–0 to Bristol City at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium). It was the club's first appearance at the National Stadium in their 127-year history.
7. The Second Division was renamed and rebranded League 1 from the start of the 2004–05 season, along with the Championship and League 2.
8. Won promotion by beating [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_F.C.) 3–2 after extra-time in the [play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Football_League_Second_Division_play-off_Final) at the [Millennium Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Stadium) in Cardiff. It was the first year the play-off finals were held in Cardiff following the closure of [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium) for redevelopment.
9. Lost 3–9 on aggregate to [Crewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewe_Alexandra_F.C.) in the play-off semi-final.
10. The Fourth Division was renamed the [Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) after the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League) was formed and broke away from the Football League.
11. Won promotion by beating [Bristol City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_City_F.C.) 4–0 in a replayed play-off final at [Fellows Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellows_Park). The original tie was played over two legs and ended 3–3 with a penalty shootout to decide the venue of the replay, which Walsall won.
12. [Associate Members' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy) introduced for third and fourth tier teams for the 1983–84 season. In 1992 it was renamed the Football League Trophy and renamed again as the EFL Trophy in 2016.
13. The [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) was introduced in the 1960–61 season specifically as a mid-week floodlit tournament, to replace the [Southern Professional Floodlit Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Professional_Floodlit_Cup).
14. The Fourth Division was created in 1958 by merging the regionalised Third Division North and South. The 12 best teams of each regional league in [1957–58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957%E2%80%9358_Football_League) remained in the nationalised Third Division and the rest, including Walsall, became founder members of the Fourth Division. This gave Walsall the distinction of being founder members of Second, Third and Fourth tiers in the Football League.
15. Walsall were forced to reapply to the Football League for the fourth season in a row ahead of the 1955–56 season and were re-elected with 33 votes. This was the tenth and final election the club had to face.
16. For the third season in a row, Walsall were forced to reapply to the Football League and were re-elected with 32 votes.
17. For the second season in a row, Walsall were forced to reapply to the Football League and were re-elected with 41 votes.
18. Forced to reapply to the Football League for the 1952–53 season and were re-elected with 45 votes.
19. The Third Division North and South Cup was revived in 1946. In each regional section 11 teams played on a league basis, with the first two places in each region then contesting the semi-finals of a knockout competition. Walsall reached the final where they lost 1–0 to [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.F.C._Bournemouth) at [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_(stadium)).
20. The FA Cup was contested in 1945–46 but the Football League did not resume until the following season.
21. 1939–40 season was abandoned after 3 league matches due to the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and all results were annulled.
22. Forced to reapply to the Football League for the 1939–40 season and were re-elected with 36 votes.
23. Forced to reapply to the Football League for the 1938–39 season but were re-elected with 34 votes.
24. Reached the final of the [Third Division North Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North_Cup), losing 2–0 to [Stockport County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockport_County_F.C.) at [Maine Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Road).
25. Forced to reapply to the Football League for the 1926–27 season but were comfortably re-elected with 33 votes.
26. The Third Division North was formed in 1921 from several regional leagues including the Birmingham & District League. Walsall were founder members and re-joined the Football League after a twenty year absence.
27. Applied for a place in the Football League in 1920–21 but were not elected.
28. Home match against Cwm Albion in April 1912 was not played as Cwm could not complete their fixtures. Walsall were awarded the win.
29. Left the Southern League at end of the 1911–12 season and continued to play in only the Birmingham & District League.
30. Elected to the Southern League Division 2 in 1910–11 but continued to play in the Birmingham & District League.
31. From 1905 the larger clubs in the region, including Walsall and other Football League clubs, were allowed to enter their reserve sides in the Birmingham Senior Cup and invariably did so. Therefore, results beyond this year are not included in the seasons records.
32. Reapplied for a place in the Football League in 1902–03 but were not elected.
33. Having finished in the bottom three, Walsall had to reapply to the Football League. They were not re-elected and replaced in the league by [Bristol City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_City_F.C.). This would begin the club's twenty year absence from league football.
34. Highest ever league position. With 18 teams in the First Division, Walsall were placed 24th nationally – the equivalent of 4th place in the modern day Championship.
35. Retained the [Birmingham Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Senior_Cup) by beating [Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.) 3–0 in the final.
36. Walsall beat [Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.) 2–1 in the final of the [Birmingham Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Senior_Cup).
37. Walsall (having dropped the Town Swifts in their name) elected back to the Football League for the 1896–97 season, joining [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool_F.C.) and [Gainsborough Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainsborough_Trinity_F.C.) in replacing [Burslem Port Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vale_F.C.), [Crewe Alexandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewe_Alexandra_F.C.) and [Rotherham Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotherham_Town_F.C._(1878)).
38. Having finished in the bottom four, Walsall Town Swifts again had to reapply for their place in the league. They were not re-elected and replaced in the league by [Loughborough Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_Town_F.C.).
39. Having finished in last place, Walsall Town Swifts had to reapply for their place in the league. They were re-elected and following expansion to 16 teams, joined in the Second Division by [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) and [Woolwich Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.).
40. The [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) was formed in 1892 largely by clubs who had been competing in the Football Alliance. Walsall Town Swifts were founder members.
41. The [Football Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Alliance) was formed in 1889 by 12 clubs as an alternative to the Football League. Walsall Town Swifts were founder members.
42. Walsall have competed in the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) every season since their formation in 1888. Both preceding clubs, Walsall Town and Walsall Swifts, also both competed in each season from 1882–83 until the amalgamation.
43. In the same year an attempt was made to set up a league called [The Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination) involving clubs not invited to join [The Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League). Lack of proper organisation meant it materialised into a series of friendly matches without proper league structure and wound up in April 1889.
44. Top goalscorer statistics include goals scored in league matches only.
45. Average attendance taken from league matches only.
46. [Birmingham Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Senior_Cup) 1888–1905, [Third Division North Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_North_Cup) 1934–36, [Third Division South Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South_Cup) 1936–46 and [EFL Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy)(under various names) 1983–present.
