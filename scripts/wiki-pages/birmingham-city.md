List of Birmingham City F.C. seasons

Small Heath F.C., champions of the inaugural [Football League Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) [1892–93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892%E2%80%9393_in_English_football)

[Birmingham City Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_Football_Club), an [association football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), England, was founded in 1875 as Small Heath Alliance. For the first thirteen years of their existence, there was no league football, so matches were arranged on an [ad hoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc) basis, supplemented by [cup competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_competition) organised at local and national level. Small Heath first entered the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup) in the [1881–82 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1881%E2%80%9382_FA_Cup), and won their first trophy, the [Walsall Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall_Senior_Cup), the following season. During the 1880s, they played between 20 and 30 matches each season.

In 1888, the club became a [limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_company_limited_by_shares) under the name of Small Heath F.C. Ltd, and joined [the Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination), a league set up to provide organised football for those clubs not invited to join [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) which was to start the same year. However, the Combination was not well organised and folded in April 1889 with many fixtures still outstanding. Small Heath were founder members of the [Football Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Alliance) in [1889–90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1889%E2%80%9390_Football_Alliance), and three years later were elected to the newly formed [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) of the Football League. They topped the table in their first season, failing to win [promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_and_relegation) via the [test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_match_(association_football)) system then in operation, but reached the top flight for the first time in 1894. They were renamed Birmingham in 1905, finally becoming Birmingham City in 1943.

Birmingham City FC Seasons

The club's official history rated [1955–56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955%E2%80%9356_Birmingham_City_F.C._season) as their best season to date. The newly promoted club achieved their highest ever finishing position of sixth in the [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division), reached the [1956 FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_FA_Cup_Final), and became the first English club side to participate in European competition when they played their opening game in the group stages of the [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup). Their only major trophy is the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Cup), which they won in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Football_League_Cup_Final) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Football_League_Cup_Final); they reached the FA Cup final twice and the final of the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup twice. During the 1990s, they twice won the [Football League Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Trophy), a competition open to clubs in the third and fourth tiers of English football, and in [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_EFL_League_One), they won the third-tier [EFL League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One) title with a league record points total of 111.

As of the end of the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Birmingham_City_F.C._season), the club's first team had spent 57 seasons in the top division of English football, 60 in the second and 5 in the third. The table details their achievements in [first-team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_team_(association_football)#F) competitions, and records their top goalscorer and average home league attendance, for each completed season since their first appearance in the [Birmingham Senior Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Senior_Cup) in 1878–79.

58. The [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup) competition began in the [1960–61 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960%E2%80%9361_in_English_football).
59. Beginning with the 1925–26 season, the FA Cup was structured so that the third round proper contained 64 teams. Prior to that date, the structure had varied, so rounds are not directly comparable to the round of the same name after 1925. For example, in 1892–93, Small Heath's first season in the Football League, there were only three rounds proper before the semifinal, as compared with the current six.
60. The [United Counties League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Counties_League_(1890s))(or United Midland Counties League) was one of several short-lived leagues of similar name. This one was established in 1894, involving ten teams from [the Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Midlands), to be played as a supplementary competition to fill vacant dates in the season without the trouble and expense of arranging friendly matches. Small Heath finished third in their four-team section in the 1893–94 season, and did not participate again.
61. Founder member of the [Football Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Alliance), which started a year after the Football League.
62. An attempt was made to set up a league called [The Combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Combination) involving clubs not invited to join the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Football_League). Lack of proper organisation meant it was wound up in April 1889 with many fixtures still outstanding. Small Heath played 11 of their full 16 fixtures.
63. Birmingham City became the first English club team to take part in European competition when they played their first group game in the [1955–58 Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955%E2%80%9358_Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup) on 15 May 1956, a goalless draw away at [Internazionale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internazionale). The competition lasted over three English seasons with the final not played until 1958. The [London XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_XI), a representative side made up of players from several London clubs, were the first English team when they played their first group game in 1955.
